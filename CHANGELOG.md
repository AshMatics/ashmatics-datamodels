### v0.16.0 (2026-09-14) — ASHFORGE-720 — the hazard record, and a risk tier that is derived
- Added the `risk` module: `HazardRecord` and the vocabularies it is scored on, contract half of aigov-framework ADR-031 D1. One record per hazardous situation carries the whole ISO 14971 chain — hazard, sequence of events with failure modes as links, harm on the four `harm_model.yaml` dimensions, P1 and P2, controls tagged with hierarchy tier and the P they reduce, verification then validation, residual position, acceptance with a revisit date, watched items. Ported from the markdown field contract in `WP-RM-01-Hazard-Analysis-Report.md`, which stays the authored end.
- **The two worked records in the framework's `Example-Hazard-Records-Two-Systems.md` are the test fixtures**, transcribed field for field (an imaging triage tool and a prior-authorization agent). ADR-031 had held this contract until the fields settled against a real analysis; J. Kalafut decided on 2026-09-14 to ship it now with those records as the proof, since they are the only records written through the whole chain, and fields can still flex in a minor bump.
- What a record validates is what is checkable from the record alone, each rule mutation-tested red: depth never shallower than the harm level earns; `hazard_record_only` needs a stop rationale; a control cannot be validated before it is verified, or dated before its verification; **a residual P1 lower than initial needs a control that reduces P1, and the same for P2**; residual harm cannot exceed scored harm; acceptance needs a residual position, is never of an unacceptable risk, and must revisit after it accepts; **"tolerable with justification and monitoring" is not accepted until `watched_items` names something**. Harm level (the max, never the mean), earned depth, failure modes and unvalidated controls are properties, not fields, so they cannot disagree with what they are read from.
- `system_risk_tier()` is ADR-031 D9's `{{system.riskTier}}` derivation, with `RiskTierOverride` (tier, reason, author, date) kept beside the derived value, never in place of it. **Per record, then the maximum** — decided 2026-09-14 against the ADR's literal wording ("the system's harm level and its maximum residual P1 × P2"), which pairs one record's harm with another's probability; a test builds exactly that case. The unit of scoring is the hazardous situation (ADR-020 D1).
- `DEFAULT_RISK_TIER_TABLE` is CHAR's first calibration, chosen the same day. P1 and P2 are ordinal, so "P1 × P2" is their ordinal positions summed (2–9) and cut unlikely 2–4 / possible 5–6 / likely 7–9. Harm 5 is high in every column and harm 4 is never low, because severity is never discounted by likelihood (RMP §4.4). The table is tested cell for cell, for monotonicity, and for the floor; the worked records derive high (imaging) and moderate (prior-auth). A record with no residual position is read at its initial position, which errs high.
- `system_harm_level()` reads the SCORED harm level, not the residual: `{{system.harmLevel}}` sets analysis depth, which is decided before controls exist. No records derives `None` for both, never a default.
- `SignalAccess` (`ash:SignalAccessScheme`, ontology 0.18.0 / 2.11.0) in `common.enums` beside `ActionAuthority`, because it is a system facet. **Its values are notations (`connected`), not local names**, because its only consumers — the CLF attribute and the surveillance plan — carry notations.
- `RiskTier` is a new enum, **not** a reuse of `RiskCategory`: that one is the FDA device-class risk (`fda.classifications`) and only its spellings coincide. The tier is derived from harm and probability and ADR-020 D8 forbids reading it as a device class. The ontology had conflated the two ("broadly equivalent to FDA Device Class I/II/III", "aligned with RiskCategory"); ashmatics-ontology 0.18.1 corrects the definitions, and two tests keep it corrected — one that the enums stay separate, one that `ash:RiskTierScheme`'s text never again describes a device class (red against ontology 0.18.0 by design).
- `methods.SystemAttribute` could not hold the registry it describes: no `integer` type (`system.harmLevel`), no `allowed_values` (eleven of fourteen attributes), no `deprecated` / `superseded_by` (`system.patientFacing`). All four added, and `tests/methods/test_clf_registry.py` now validates every registered attribute from the sibling framework checkout through the contract and checks harmLevel, signalAccess and riskTier against their enums. Nothing had ever run the registry through it, which is how the drift stayed invisible.
- Fixed `artifacts.ToolRef`, whose fields were deleted in the 0.11.0 docstring rewrite: every release since shipped a `ToolRef` that accepts no fields, and the two artifact tests had been red on main since.
- `__version__` in `__init__.py` was still `0.12.0`; it now moves with `pyproject.toml`.
- Requires `ashmatics-ontology >= 0.18.1` for the binding guards (`SignalAccessScheme`, and the corrected `RiskTierScheme` text).

### v0.15.0 (2026-09-12) — ASHFORGE-370 — the seventh method applicability axis
- Added `action_authority` to `ApplicabilityProfile`, bound to `ash:ActionAuthorityScheme` via `ashcai:appliesToActionAuthority`. Method applicability could not condition on what a system may do without a human in the loop, though failure-mode applicability has since 0.10.0. A system that acts closes a feedback loop with no human step in between, which changes which methods are *meaningful*, not merely which are convenient.
- **`ActionAuthority` moved from `failure_modes.enums` to `common.enums`, and is re-exported so nothing breaks.** It classifies the SYSTEM, not the thing conditioning on it; it lived in `failure_modes` only because failure-mode applicability was the first consumer. Two modules condition on it now, and a third would otherwise have to import a system facet from an unrelated module. Every existing `from ashmatics_datamodels.failure_modes import ActionAuthority` keeps working.
- The re-export is load-bearing but reads as an unused import, and `ruff --fix` deleted it once during the move — which would have broken those imports silently. It now carries a `# noqa: F401` and, more to the point, `tests/common/test_action_authority_home.py` asserts all four import paths yield the same enum object. Comments can be removed; tests argue back.
- Requires `ashmatics-ontology >= 0.16.0`, cut for this release. `ashcai:appliesToActionAuthority` did not exist — the six sibling axes each realize an `ashcai:methodAppliesTo` subproperty and this one had none. It was minted rather than left to a scheme-only annotation: the binding guard accepts either, so the field could have shipped with no property and would then have been the one axis of seven missing it, for no reason anyone could later name.
- Verified rather than assumed: breaking either the scheme IRI or the property IRI fails `tests/methods/test_ontology_binding.py::test_property_iris_resolve`, so the guard demonstrably covers the new axis on both annotations.

### v0.14.0 (2026-09-12) — ASHFORGE-370 — per-member method provenance, derived
- Added `member_provenance()` in a new `methods/derive.py`, plus the two-value `MemberProvenance` enum. This is aigov-framework ADR-022 **D6**: `delivery_mode` is set-level and cannot answer "why this method", but an auditor's "why is this the bias method for this system" has a materially different answer per member, and only the exemplar carries Asher's warrant.
- **Derived, not stored, and that is the whole design.** D6 says so in its own text: the adjudicated origin is "already expressible today as a `MethodSetAdjustment` with its mandatory `rationale`", and the exemplar origin is membership in the inherited `members` baseline. Both facts are already written; what was missing is the per-member *view*, which is a join rather than a field. Storing it would give "where did this method come from" two answers that can disagree — the same defect `registry.derive.system_class()` was built to avoid in 0.12.0, in this same library, for this same reason.
- **`resolved_members` is unchanged**, so this release is additive and nothing downstream re-pins. A test asserts `ApprovedMethodSet` grows no `provenance`/`origin` field, so the second stored answer cannot appear later without someone reading D6 first.
- **Two values where D6 names three origins.** Operator-selected-for-this-job is, by D5, recorded against the job in the customer's datastore and never claimed by CHAR, so it cannot occur in an org's standing approved set; no contract in this library represents a job record today, so a third enum member would be a value nothing could produce. When that contract lands it adds the third. This is a deliberate gap, like `REGISTRY_ADMISSIBLE_KINDS` in 0.13.0, not an omission.
- Three behaviours worth knowing, each mutation-tested red before being left green: an adjustment with `phase: both` confers adjudicated provenance under **both** concrete phase keys (read naively, a member added for both phases reads as an exemplar under one of them); only `added` is an origin, since `reranked` leaves a member whatever it was and `removed` members are absent from `resolved_members`; and the baseline wins when a member is both, because a re-added exemplar still came from Asher.
- An unattributable member raises `ValueError` rather than defaulting, matching the registry derivations' fail-loudly posture. A silent default would make an unattributable member indistinguishable from a warranted one, which is the confusion D6 exists to prevent. Callers rendering an artifact should expect it; in exchange the derivation doubles as a consistency check on the set.
- No ontology change: `MemberProvenance` is contract-level vocabulary with no scheme, and ADR-022 does not ask for one.

### v0.13.0 (2026-09-12) — ASHFORGE-370 — tool substantiation kinds and source classes
- Added `SubstantiationKind` (`ashcai:SubstantiationKindScheme`) and `SourceClass` (`ashcai:SourceClassScheme`) to the `controls` module, beside the five axes already there. These anchor `admits` and `reads_from` on all 135 entries of the aigov-framework's `tooling_registry.yaml`, which were gated as free strings against hard-coded sets in `validate_tool_contracts.py` — the same stopgap the method registry ran under before `GovernanceMethodScheme`.
- **`REGISTRY_ADMISSIBLE_KINDS` is the scheme minus `attest`, and the mismatch is the point.** The scheme and the enum carry five kinds; the registry's `admits` field legally carries four, because every contract admits `attest` by construction and writing it is redundant. `attest` is still a real value everywhere downstream — it is the default binding for an unbuilt connector and the kind a `ToolBinding` or an `ArtifactRecord` most often carries — so it belongs in the vocabulary and not in that one field. A consumer validating registry content checks membership in `REGISTRY_ADMISSIBLE_KINDS`, never in `SubstantiationKind`. A test asserts the gap stays exactly one concept and exactly that one, in both directions, so neither half can be "reconciled" into the other.
- `YIELDS_EVIDENCE_MECHANISM` mirrors `ashcai:yieldsEvidenceMechanism`. Unlike `YIELDS_ASSURANCE_MODE` it is one-to-many, and the guard checks it as an exact SET per kind rather than a subset — a subset assertion would let the ontology grow an edge the ceiling calculation never sees. This bridge is why the kinds were made to mirror the mechanisms at all: a contract admits a kind, the kind yields mechanisms, the mechanisms already carry assurance modes, so the achievable ceiling stays one function instead of two.
- `SUBSTANTIATION_ORDINAL` is the ladder attest (1) to connect (5), **not** the order values appear in a registry entry's `admits` list. 11 of the 135 entries author that list out of ladder order, so the sequence there is incidental; had the ordinal been derived from it, every consumer comparing the two would have been quietly wrong with nothing failing.
- `SourceClass` is a parallel axis to `SubstantiationKind`, not a refinement beneath it, and that was measured rather than assumed: 8 of the 17 classes are reached by both `compute` and `connect` contracts, so source class varies independently of how a claim is substantiated. Its grain is coarse by decision (J. Kalafut, 2026-09-12) — grouped by the connection a customer would actually make, not by what a tool is called, so `LEARNING_PLATFORM` spans LMS, assessment, competency and certification. Flat by decision too: a hierarchy would let a consumer roll up at a grain nobody chose, and a test asserts no concept carries `skos:broader`.
- No value in `SourceClass` names a product or a system a customer runs. The field name invites exactly that, and a named system is instance data belonging in the coreapp Fabric binding's `target`.
- Two tests guard what the ontology must NOT have: the flat-scheme check (which asserts its concept count first, so an unresolvable scheme IRI cannot pass as an empty loop) and a tripwire that no `readsFromSourceClass` property exists. Which tool reads from what is registry content held on the tool entry in CHAR; minting that property would invite asserting instance relations in TTL.
- Requires `ashmatics-ontology >= 0.15.0` (`owl:versionInfo` 2.8.0), cut for this release. `tests/controls/test_ontology_binding.py` resolves the TTL from a sibling checkout or `ASHMATICS_ONTOLOGY_DIR`, so the guard reads whatever is checked out rather than a pinned artifact — the tag is what makes that checkout reproducible.

### v0.12.0 (2026-09-07) — ASHFORGE-659 — CHAR's system class, as a derivation
- Added `SystemClass` (`clinical`, `administrative-operational`) and `derive.system_class()`. This is the contract half of aigov-framework ADR-015 D6 step 1: CHAR splits its corpus into a scope-neutral **core** and **system classes** that specialize it, and `{{system.class}}` is the CLF selector that picks which body of content applies to a given AI system.
- **It is derived from `RegistryCategory`, not stored.** The registry already answers this question — the three-way category triad, BOUND to `ash:ScopeZoneScheme` — and `is_clinical_use` already calls the clinical edge "the rule-bearing edge (clinical vs. everything else)". Adding a second stored field for the same fact would give "is this system clinical?" two answers that can disagree. So `system_class` is the second reading of one stored value: `CLINICAL → clinical`; `OPERATIONAL` and `ADMINISTRATIVE` both → `administrative-operational`. A test asserts the two derivations never disagree about which side of the clinical edge an entry falls on.
- The 3→2 collapse is ADR-015 open item 1's deliberate single id: whether revenue cycle, supply chain and facilities diverge enough to split is a question only the second class's content plan can answer, and splitting now would encode a guess in every `class_applicability` array in CHAR. Because the triad stays stored, a later split **widens this function** and re-classifies no entry — which is the whole reason the collapse lives here rather than in the customer's data.
- `None` (uncharacterized entry) derives `None`, never a class. CHAR content defaults to `core` in the absence of a class (ADR-015 D3.1); returning the majority value would silently apply one class's specializations to a system nobody has classified.
- Values are kebab-case, unlike the snake_case everywhere else in the module. That is ADR-015 D4's "one canonical spelling" rule for class ids, enforced on the CHAR side by `validate_registry.py`; the vocabulary freeze test is the contract half.
- Declared `PENDING` in `registry/bindings.py` — **deferred, not declined.** ADR-015 open item 5 holds `ashcai:SystemClassScheme` until the second class's ids are final, because a `skos:notation` fixed in the data plane (ADR-006) is expensive to split. Unlike the other derived vocabularies, which are PRODUCT by design, this one wants an anchor eventually, so PENDING keeps the gap visible in every test run. Mint it under `SystemClassScheme` — *not* `GovernanceProfileScheme`: "profile" is the aigov-framework ADR-016 word for a controls derivation per engagement driver, and the two senses already collided once.
- Naming trap worth carrying downstream: `{{system.class}}` sits beside `{{system.modelClass}}` in the CLF `system` scope and they are unrelated axes — `modelClass` is the *technique* (cnn, transformer), `class` is *what the system does for the organization*. ADR-015's Consequences section names reviewer confusion between them as a real failure mode.
- No coreapp migration: nothing new is stored, so `core/models/ai_registry.py` and `aiRegistryTaxonomies.ts` are unchanged.

### v0.10.0 (2026-09-04) — ASHFORGE-604 — the AI failure mode taxonomy contracts
- Added the `failure_modes` module: `AIFailureMode`, the ten-class vocabulary for **how a governed AI system fails**, bound to `ash:AIFailureModeScheme` (minted in ontology 0.14.0 / v2.7.0 for this release). CHAR had no content for "how does this class of AI fail, and what follows from that failure" — zero matches for fail / degrade / mode / robust / reliability / behaviour across all eleven process domains' base practices, so a site convening subject matter experts got a method name and a blank page.
- `FailureModeTaxonomy` is the document contract for the aigov-framework `failure_mode_taxonomy.yaml`, the way `MethodRegistry` is for `method_registry.yaml`. Per aigov-framework ADR-018 D3 the taxonomy is **reference content resolved at SOP junctions**, not a base practice and not a method: `rm-wizard-config.yaml` RM-2-Q5 keeps offering FMEA, FTA, HAZOP, PHA and STAMP/STPA, and this is what the chosen method is applied to.
- `Repercussion` carries the half that makes a taxonomy usable beside a hazard analysis — what an operator, an affected person or the organization actually experiences — with **separate clinical and non-clinical framings** and a validator refusing an audience that has neither. Non-clinical AI is in scope by design: an administrative scheduling system fails differently and the repercussion is not patient harm.
- `FailureModeApplicability` reuses the ash facet enums with the same zero-free-string rule as method applicability. It differs in one way worth knowing: an omitted axis means *unrestricted*, because most failure classes are reachable by most systems and the axes exist to exclude the ones that are not (confabulation has no meaning for a system emitting a category).
- `ActionAuthority` surfaces here for the first time (`ash:ActionAuthorityScheme`, ontology 0.13.0 / ASHFORGE-638) because failure-mode applicability conditions on it: a system that acts closes a feedback loop with no human step in between.
- Requires `ashmatics-ontology >= 0.14.0`. `tests/failure_modes/test_ontology_binding.py` checks all ten concepts resolve, and its reverse-completeness check is **load-bearing here rather than advisory** — `AIFailureModeScheme` is a facet scheme, not an open registry like `GovernanceMethodScheme`, so a class minted in the ontology with no enum member means the taxonomy cannot express it.

### v0.9.0 (2026-09-02) — ASHFORGE-627 — controls axis vocabularies and the platform availability record
- Added the `controls` module: the five CHAR control axes as ontology-bound enums (`ControlMaturity`, `AssuranceMode`, `EvidenceMechanism`, `MechanismAvailability`, `AssuranceCadence`), each value the `skos:notation` of its `ashcai:*Scheme` concept, with the ordinal tables and the `yieldsAssuranceMode` edges mirrored and guard-checked both directions against the TTL (`tests/controls/test_ontology_binding.py`). `Edition` (essentials / enterprise, coreapp ADR-039) rides along as PRODUCT vocabulary with a guard that fires if a scheme for it ever lands.
- `MechanismAvailabilityRecord` is the contract for the platform's mechanism-availability record, which lives in **asher-infra** (`platform-capabilities/mechanism-availability.yaml`) and is pinned by release manifests — the second of the three gates in ontology ADR-009's assurance-ceiling design, and until now the one with a ratified vocabulary and no assignments anywhere. Per mechanism family and per edition, exactly one entry per family, a mandatory `note`, `provided_by` as manifest `repos:` keys, and `depends_on_any` for the computed_derivation conjunction rule. Essentials may be split by the governed system's deployment mode (`DeploymentMode`, bound to `ash:DeploymentModeScheme`) because a hosted-only edition's reach to a source is structural; Enterprise is one value. `is_confirmed` tells a consumer whether the owner has ruled on the values.
- The ceiling itself ships as pure functions on the record so coreapp and the framework share one implementation: `achievable_ceiling(declared, edition, entitled)` and `diagnose_gap(target, …) -> GapCause` (availability → product backlog, entitlement → licensing, customer → their governance roadmap). The customer gate is "entitled AND reachable": whether a site's IT will open the path is that gate, not availability and not the customer's governance work. `beta` is the default threshold at which a mechanism counts; it is a parameter, not a constant.
- Requires `ashmatics-ontology >= 0.12.0` (the ADR-009 schemes; cut 2026-09-02 for this release). The binding guard skips without a sibling checkout and fails against v0.11.0. Of the same ontology work, `EngagementScope` and `RiskTierScheme` still have no contract here; `DeploymentModeScheme` is now bound via `DeploymentMode`. The catalog and profile document contracts (the `MethodRegistry` equivalent for `ashc_controls_catalog.yaml`) remain queued; this module ships the vocabularies they will share.

Same stored-fact/derived-judgment philosophy as `clinical_use`. Downstream: coreapp's sourcing picker and SRS-REG-15a wording must move to channel values (dev-only data so far).
- NOT bound, deliberately: `ash:OperationalPurposeScheme` — explicitly a seed vocabulary, nothing consumes purpose yet (note its `prior_authorization_ops` notation is distinct from the clinical scheme's `prior_authorization`).
